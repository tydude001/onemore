# Returning to the gym with a healing radial head fracture

Not medical advice. This is a research record, not a treatment plan: it exists because
the `comeback` program's movement selection is driven by a specific injury, and a program
that filters exercises for a medical reason must say which reason and cite it, or the
filter becomes folklore the next time someone edits the module.

This document is written in the third person about a lifter with this injury. It does not
replace a treating clinician. What it does is take the restrictions and aggravators
typically documented for a nonoperative radial head fracture and work out which gym
movements they implicate. A real case record — imaging, exam findings, appointment
history — is a private, personal document and is deliberately not reproduced here; only
the literature and the reasoning it licenses are.

---

## What the literature says, and what it licenses

### 1. The radial head carries most of the axial load through the elbow

Cadaveric loading studies put radiocapitellar transmission at roughly **50–60 %** of the
axial load crossing the elbow (Morrey et al.'s classic figure; reproduced in the modern
review below). So any movement that drives force up the forearm into the elbow loads a
fractured radial head directly, in proportion to how hard it is pushed.

- Smithson K, Smith J, Hogue W, Mannen E, Ahmadi S. *Biomechanics of axial load
  transmission across the native human elbow.* Shoulder & Elbow, 2021.
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8600677/>
- Morrey BF, An KN, Stormont TJ. *Force transmission through the radial head.* JBJS 1988.
  <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3343271/>
- Kaas L et al. / Klug A et al. *Maximizing outcomes in the treatment of radial head
  fractures.* J Orthop Traumatol 2019.
  <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6431334/>

### 2. That load peaks near full extension — 0 to 30° of flexion

The same axial-load work finds **the greatest force transmission between 0 and 30° of
flexion, decreasing consistently as the elbow flexes further**.

This is the finding that does the most work in the program, for two reasons:

- It gives a mechanism for a commonly reported aggravator, *pushing up from a chair or
  couch* — an axial load through a nearly-straight arm.
- It converts into an unambiguous gym instruction: **stop pressing short of lockout.**
  Not "press lighter" — press through the *flexed* part of the range, which is where the
  radiocapitellar joint is least loaded, and leave out the last 30°, which is where it is
  most loaded. That costs almost nothing in training effect and removes the peak.

### 3. Forearm rotation shifts the load, but the evidence conflicts on magnitude

Two lines of evidence disagree, and the disagreement is recorded here rather than
resolved, because the program does not depend on it:

- **In vivo 3-D CT** (Shioya et al., JSES 2020) found **no significant difference in mean
  radiocapitellar contact force between pronation and supination.** What changed was
  contact *area* and its centre, which shifted anteriorly from pronation to supination.
  <https://www.jshoulderelbow.org/article/S1058-2746(20)30737-0/abstract>
- **Cadaveric axial loading** found force transmission **consistently greater in
  pronation than supination.**
  <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1058274617306638>

**Consequence for the program: grip selection is a comfort call, not a load-bearing
design claim.** Neutral grips are offered wherever a machine has them, and the lifter is
told to use whichever hurts less. Nothing in the program's logic depends on grip.

What *is* uncontested and does matter is that **loaded rotation is a documented
aggravator for a fracture at this stage** — a clinical commonplace, not an inference —
so movements whose resistance acts through supination or pronation are out of block 1.

### 4. Cubital tunnel pressure rises with elbow flexion

A positive Tinel's sign at the cubital tunnel is a common finding alongside a radial head
fracture. Elbow flexion **decreases the tunnel's cross-sectional area by about 35 % at
135°**, increases pressure within it, and simultaneously puts traction on the nerve (its
own cross-section narrowing 35–40 %).

- Gelberman RH et al. *The cubital tunnel: anatomic, histologic, and biomechanical
  study.* J Shoulder Elbow Surg 1998. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10543601/>
- AAOS OrthoInfo, *Ulnar nerve entrapment at the elbow.*
  <https://orthoinfo.aaos.org/en/diseases--conditions/ulnar-nerve-entrapment-at-the-elbow/>

The same mechanism matches a commonly reported aggravator — *sitting with arms folded* is
sustained deep flexion. **Consequence: no loaded deep flexion.** That rules out
preacher-style curls specifically (the elbow is at its most flexed exactly where the load
is heaviest) and argues against resting between sets with the elbow held bent.

### 5. Where a lifter in this position sits on the standard nonoperative timeline

Nonoperative radial head protocols run roughly: 0–6 weeks motion and near-zero load,
6–12 weeks weaning and continued motion, **12–16 weeks generalised strengthening as
tolerated once ROM is adequate and the fracture is healed.**

- Hull University Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust, *Radial head or neck fracture — advice
  regarding healing and recovery.*
  <https://www.hey.nhs.uk/patient-leaflet/radial-head-or-neck-fracture-advice-regarding-healing-and-recovery/>
- *Rehabilitation protocol: nonoperative radial head fracture.*
  <https://www.lindsaysportsmed.com/pdf/nonop-radial-head.pdf>
- *Radial head fractures in adults: a narrative review.*
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12766341/>

A lifter early in the strengthening phase, especially one who has had a second fall or
any interval displacement, straddles the boundary between weaning and generalised
strengthening — which is the argument for the upper-body ramp being genuinely slow rather
than nominally cautious. The literature also warns specifically against jumping load:
*"progressing too quickly from lifting a cup of water to a heavy shopping bag ... can
result in overloading of soft tissue and delay recovery."*

## The movement filter this produces

Four rules, each traceable to a numbered finding above or to a commonly documented
aggravator:

| Rule | From | Effect on the program |
|---|---|---|
| Stop pressing short of lockout | §1, §2 + "pushing up from a couch" | Every press slot carries the note; no lockout-focused work at all |
| No loaded supination/pronation | documented aggravator (§3) | No supinated curls; hammer/neutral or machine only |
| No loaded deep flexion (past ~120°) | §4 + "arms folded" | Preacher curl dropped; deep-stretch positions avoided |
| Nothing that risks a fall or a carry | §5 + the risk a fall poses to a healing fracture | No Smith bar on the back, no heavy dumbbell carries to a bench |

### Movements dropped from `rebuild` for this reason, and why

- **`captains_chair_knee_raise`** — reads as core work, but it suspends bodyweight through
  the forearms with the elbows flexed and pressure directly over the olecranon region.
  Two contraindications at once (§4, and an olecranon/coronoid fracture, when present).
  Replaced with `crunch_machine` / `decline_crunch`.
- **`preacher_curl`** — §4, above. The one curl variant that maximises load at maximum
  flexion.
- **`tricep_pushdown`, `triceps_extension_machine`, `skullcrusher`** — direct triceps work
  pulls on the olecranon, and the pushdown finishes in terminal extension, the 0–30°
  window of §2. Deferred entirely in block 1; the presses train the triceps anyway.
- **`barbell_bicep_curl`, `bicep_curl`** — supinated. Replaced with `cable_hammer_curl`
  (neutral throughout).
- **`dumbbell_bench_press`** — carrying the dumbbells to the bench is the documented
  "carrying heavier objects" aggravator, and the bottom position is deep flexion under
  load. Deferred; machine pressing covers the pattern.
- **`smith_squat`** — nothing to do with the legs and everything to do with holding a
  loaded bar on the back: a sustained hard grip with the elbows flexed (§4), under a bar,
  with a fall risk that a healing fracture cannot afford (§5). Dropped from block 1. Leg
  press gives the squat pattern with zero arm involvement.

### What is deliberately *not* done

- **No rehab is prescribed.** Any clinician-directed home exercise program stays the
  clinician's; the training program does not duplicate, modify or comment on it.
- **No pain rule in the engine.** Pain is not in the Strong export and would have to be
  guessed at; per PLAN.md § RPE is not logged, a rule may not depend on a signal that is
  not in the data. Pain guidance is printed text on the rendered week. If the lifter backs
  off a load, `tm_reduce` reads it as a miss and lowers the training max — which is the
  correct response, and self-correcting.
- **No upper-body block 2.** Writing many weeks of upper-body progression across a
  scheduled clinical reassessment would be planning past the checkpoint that could change
  everything. Block 1 is written to land on it instead.
